Positive Chemotropism

After a pollen grain germinates on the stigma, its tube grows down through the style's transmitting tissue. Synergid cells flanking the egg cell release chemoattractant molecules — including LURE peptides and a calcium (Ca²⁺) gradient — that form a concentration gradient highest near the micropyle.

The pollen tube senses this gradient via receptor-like kinases and bends toward the source, ensuring the two male gametes (sperm cells) reach the embryo sac for double fertilization — one sperm fusing with the egg to form the embryo, the other with the polar nuclei to form endosperm.

The control tube (purple) grows straight through the style with no chemical guidance and exits the ovary without reaching the micropyle. The guided tube (orange) curves progressively as the attractant gradient steepens inside the ovary.

Double Fertilization

The pollen tube tip ruptures inside a synergid, releasing its two non-motile sperm cells — the male gametes — right beside the egg apparatus.

One sperm fuses with the egg cell (n + n) to form the diploid zygote, the future embryo.

The other fuses with the two polar nuclei (n + n + n) to form the triploid primary endosperm nucleus, which develops into nutritive endosperm tissue.
